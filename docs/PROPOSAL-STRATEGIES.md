# How to write for different funding schemes

**Different researchers also need different research arguments.** The aim is to identify the strongest truthful connection between this applicant's distinctive academic path, a promising next research question and a specific funder's purpose—not to reuse one attractive AI-written narrative for everyone.

**Different calls need different research arguments.** The applicant's CV is a trusted input *only for observed and user-confirmed facts*, not a justification for generic grant text. A funder's current-round form, review criteria, project restrictions and exact section limits override every pattern below. First use `/match` to check eligibility, then `/prepare` to choose the argument, then `/apply` to draft each required section.

## Writing matrix

| Scheme | Central evaluator question | Best-fit narrative order | Evidence to gather before writing | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent / postdoctoral fellowship | **Why this applicant, at this career stage?** Can they develop an independent programme? | Research vision → independently owned contribution → aims → feasibility → host/mentorship → career development | CV and candidate contributions, real publication roles, prospective host/mentor support if actually provided, thesis/viva/award dates, realistic career path | Repackaging the supervisor's project or assuming thesis submission equals a PhD award |
| PI-led research project grant | **What new knowledge, by what testable method?** | Unmet problem → closest baselines → specific hypothesis/method → experiments → work packages → resourcing/risks | Primary literature, preliminary data only if actually observed, reproducible research plan, costed roles/salary and technical feasibility | Generic AI claims without a discriminating experiment or claiming an unconfirmed PI agreed |
| Collaborative/consortium grant | **Why must these particular partners collaborate?** | Shared mission → each partner's unique capability → work packages and dependencies → coordination → outputs/impact | Host eligibility, consortium/partner requirements, letters of intent where required, role ownership, cofunding, project governance | Describing several labs as a "consortium" without genuine agreed roles or collaborations |
| Industry/innovation/translation | **What real problem will be solved or adopted?** | Beneficiary need → current gap → delivery mechanism/prototype → technical and adoption milestones → deployment/impact → IP/risk | Verified user need, partner feedback, actual TRL if the scheme uses it, budget/eligible costs, IP/commercialization rules and implementation milestones | Selling high-level novelty without adoption evidence, or inventing an industrial partner |
| Impact/engagement prize | **What impact has already been demonstrated?** | Real change → beneficiaries → verifiable evidence → contribution/attribution → sustained benefit | Independent adoption signals, user testimonials, policy/industrial changes where evidenced, measurement denominator and causal limits | Treating publications, GitHub stars or *future* promised impact as proof of external change |

## Why the /prepare phase matters

For every scheme, `/prepare` produces a **research argument map**, not just headings. The AI should articulate the alternative routes, specific hypothesis or impact claim, key applicant evidence, strongest prior alternative, evaluation/validation plan, host/PI authority, mandatory annexes, budget mechanism and what evidence could falsify its own framing. For a fellowship, independence and applicant development are central; for a project grant, the methodology and measurable contribution are central; for an impact award, **demonstrated past outcomes** control the narrative.

Examples of critical questions:
- Is this proposal scientifically distinct from the applicant's existing PhD group? Does the new PI own the work or only host it?
- Is the funding awarded to the applicant or to a university/PI, and can staff salary be included?
- Does the funder require a submitted dissertation, passed viva, awarded degree, or employment status as of an exact date?
- Are the research milestones technically feasible with the available people, equipment and budget?
- Which specific section and evidence satisfy each published assessment criterion?
- What result would make the proposed scientific hypothesis untenable, and what is the fallback plan?

## Draft review procedure

1. **Requirements check:** official programme round, named role/host, funder and internal deadline, obligatory criteria, eligibility, budget/salary rates, format, word/page count and required attachments.
2. **Scientific challenge:** closest prior work, alternative mechanism, novelty boundaries, real vs proposed findings, strong baselines, research feasibility, matched resources and risks.
3. **Applicant evidence:** every claimed outcome/research contribution from the CV, published paper or user-confirmed source. External partner commitments require actual permission.
4. **Output review:** render the actual DOCX/PDF/LaTeX or portal text with supported tools. Audit overflow, references, tables, spelling, document requirements and omission of unresolved evidence.
5. **Human approval:** applicant and required PI/Host/grants office sign off. A `draft_ready` status is not `submitted`, and a submission receipt is not an award.

## Example: the same researcher, two different aims

*Illustrative ideas for a researcher working on AI agents; these are not validated funding calls.*

**Fellowship:** "I will develop independently verifiable methods for autonomous AI research, identify what constitutes evidence of improvement, and build an independent team with an eligible mentor/host. The proposed work has three aims and a career-development plan."

**PI-led project:** "We hypothesize that a source-verifiable trajectory model reduces unsupported agent conclusions under matched compute budgets. We will test against specified retrieval-and-verification baselines on prespecified tasks and report both supported and negative results."

Both must refer to **actual uploaded CV facts and real published sources**. Do not invent prior project adoption, theorem proofs, experimental improvements or partner consent.
