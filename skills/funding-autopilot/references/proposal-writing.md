# Funder-specific proposal writing and review

## 1. Contract first, words second

Retrieve the current official scheme guidance and application form, not a previous year's template. Build a requirements matrix: criterion ID, required section/form field, allowed words/pages/chars, assessment weight if published, mandatory attachment, applicant/host responsibility, linked official excerpt, draft destination and remaining gap.

If the proposed applicant cannot currently satisfy a mandatory role or host requirement, label the writing an **exploratory concept only**, not a submission-ready grant. For a new PI, clarify who is named lead/fellow/employee, what research they own and what budget can legally fund the applicant. Do not promise appointment.

## 2. Research contribution, grounded in sources

Use this chain to shape the narrative (from Research Autopilot's evidence-oriented method):
**measurable problem and unmet need → closest existing solutions and evidence → precise gap → objective and hypotheses → specific work packages and discriminating evaluation → risks/contingencies → expected outputs and beneficiaries.**

For methodological/fundamental proposals, make the mathematical or algorithmic novelty explicit if relevant: assumptions, information available to the method, mechanism, rival explanation, what experimental outcome would falsify the advantage, strongest compatible baseline and resource feasibility. Do not manufacture a theorem, pilot result, ablation or impact. For non-methodological grants, use the funder's stated outcome logic rather than forcing an ML-paper structure.

Maintain claim-to-evidence mapping for every factual statement. **CV facts** require a CV location or user confirmation; **literature claims** require a real paper/DOI with proposition checked; **grant eligibility/format** needs official call; **collaboration/host commitments** require an actual consent; **pilot results** require traceable executed evidence. New planned outputs are clearly marked as projections, not accomplished work.

## 3. Deliverable-specific documents

- Project synopsis/title and lay summary/abstract at exact limits.
- Case for support: specific aims, state of art, methodology, feasibility, milestones, risks and contingency, data management/open science, ethics/security, impact/pathway, leadership and independence.
- Budget: staff time × eligible salary/rates, employer costs, consumables, travel, indirects/overhead, matching and ineligible costs; use institution-approved values instead of guesses. Explain salary support vs institutional allocation. Never assume 100% funding when programme uses fEC reimbursement.
- New PI/host packet: short tailored research pitch, why this research group, roles, mutual research benefit, call eligibility, salary mechanism, and what host must approve.
- Checklist: annexes, institutional sign-off, finance review, host letter, referee letters, letters of support, ethics/regulatory approval when required, research-data plan, reviewer rubric and upload portal.
- Optional outreach email is a **draft**, not sent.

## 4. Mandatory red-team loop

**Compliance review:** compare every section directly against call wording, page/word limits, exact roles, forms, eligibility, open dates, budget categories, internal deadlines, annexes and sign-offs. The helper script checks a subset; it does not verify funder policy.

**Scientific review:** rank strongest objections by materiality; check literature and novelty boundaries, alternate explanations, tractability, resources, named partners' actual commitments, future milestones and the evaluator's selection criteria. For each objection either repair with evidence, narrow an overstated claim, or mark unresolved. Do not hide critical gaps in appendix text.

Re-run checks after every substantial edit. Keep a versioned record containing proposal version, official rule version, CV source hash, changed claims and outstanding blockers. A compliant draft still needs human/PI/host review before submission.

## Explicit no-go claims

Do not state "funded", "submitted", "approved", "eligible" or "host committed" without direct evidence. Do not claim a draft is automatically submitted. Do not treat academic publications alone as demonstrated public engagement, market adoption, clinical benefit or policy impact. An unsuccessful application is a valid outcome; preserve feedback to improve future drafts.
